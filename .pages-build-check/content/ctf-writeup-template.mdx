## Start with scope

State the event or lab, the challenge name, and the rules that allowed you to interact with it. Avoid publishing challenge material when the organizer's rules prohibit it. Keep real systems and personal data out of a write-up.

## Record observations before conclusions

Write down what you observed, what you tried, and what changed. A concise timeline helps another learner distinguish evidence from assumptions. Redact tokens, credentials, and private identifiers even when they appear in a lab.

## Explain the turning point

Describe the clue that changed your approach and why it mattered. Include only the commands or code that are necessary to reproduce the step, and explain the important parts instead of pasting a long terminal transcript.

## Verify and close responsibly

Confirm the result inside the challenge environment, then summarize the concept you learned and one safe mitigation or defensive lesson. Do not reuse a CTF technique against a real target without explicit authorization.

> This is a write-up framework, not a report of a specific event or result. A challenge-specific account can be added after a verified CTF experience.
